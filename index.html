Learning Design in Practice

These four projects cover authentic practice, custom interaction, reusable production systems, and AI-assisted quality assurance. Together they show both the learner-facing craft and the systems thinking behind how I build, scale, and evaluate learning.

  1. 01 Authentic practice · Performance support

    Patron Media Digitization: a Playable SOP

    I reimagined a static workplace SOP as both guided practice and just-in-time performance support. Staff can work a full shift end to end at a simulated digitization desk, or jump straight to the one step they're unsure about while a patron waits. The second patron's tape turns out to be a copyrighted broadcast, so the learner has to notice that the procedure no longer applies and decide how to say no without leaving the patron feeling blamed.

  2. 02 Agentic AI workflow · QA

    Persona QA Tool

    Persona testing asks whether a build still works for someone who is rushed, new to the topic, or in a situation the design didn't plan for. This tool drafts a panel of test personas from a project description, then exports briefs or a prompt that has an AI model play one persona through the real build. The AI reports what it ran into; a person decides what counts.

    How it works

    1. Describe the project and its key moments.
    2. AI drafts six test personas.
    3. AI plays one through the real build; a person relays each action.
    4. A person scores every finding against a rubric.

  3. 03 Reusable system · AI-enabled production

    AI Do's & Don'ts: Style Guide + Activity Builder

    A short workplace module on using AI responsibly, built so the next module is quicker to make. The style guide documents the decisions the module actually runs on, from color tokens and a single feedback pattern to the art direction behind its AI-generated illustrations. An activity builder turns those same components into a self-contained starter file, so consistency comes from the system and design time goes to the scenarios and feedback.

  4. 04 Custom interaction · Visual learning

    Unfold: 3D Shapes & Nets

    This concept explores how direct manipulation can make an abstract spatial idea easier to understand. Rather than explaining 3D shapes through static diagrams, learners rotate solids, unfold them into nets, find faces, edges and corners by tapping the shape itself, and measure a face by hand before any formula appears.
